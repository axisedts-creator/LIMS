## How to use this glossary

1. **This file defines words. It never sets values.** Every number, threshold or legal statement here is copied from 04 and carries 04's status label. If you need a value that is not here, go to 04. Never take a number from this file that 04 does not also contain.
2. **Status labels** are the ones in 04 and `00-README.md`:
   - **VERIFIED**: checked against a named source.
   - **UNVERIFIED**: code must not hard-depend on it.
   - **DECISION**: engineering choice made in this documentation set; changeable only via CR.
   - **BLOCKED (B#)**: waiting on an outside party (see `07-BLOCKERS.md`).
3. **DECISION (G)** marks a meaning chosen *in this glossary* because real labs use the word in different ways. Our docs and code use only the meaning given here. If a chunk's `05-SCHEMA.md` needs a different model, do not pick a side: file a CR (`04-CHANGE-REQUESTS.md`).
4. **"In our system" column:**
   - Module names are Postgres schema names from CM §3 (`frontdesk`, `lab`, `billing`, ...).
   - Table names are the ones CM §3 lists in its notes column. The exact DDL names come from C0 (`db/migrations`).
   - Events and ports are spelled exactly as in CM §5.1.
5. Sections are grouped by domain. Terms are alphabetical inside each section. Synonyms are listed in the term cell and point to one entry.

## A. Patients, orders and commercial (front desk)

| Term | Definition | In our system | Don't confuse with / pitfalls |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Affiliation** (affiliated hospital/doctor) | A standing commercial relationship between the lab and an outside party (hospital, clinic, doctor) that sends patients or samples. Usually comes with its own price list and sometimes credit terms. 00 E2 lists "affiliated hospitals" among the masters. | Master data in `catalog` (C3: "doctors, affiliations/credit parties, price lists"). Selected at registration to decide pricing and billing. | Not every affiliation is a credit party (some only get a discounted price list and still pay cash). Not a B2B partner-portal account (C13) unless that partner is also onboarded to the portal. |
| **B2B** (business-to-business) | Work that comes to the lab through another organisation: a referring lab, hospital, clinic, collection-centre franchise or corporate client. Usually priced from that organisation's price list and often billed monthly on credit. | Credit-party billing (C4); partner portal (C13, Phase 2) with partner users, sample manifests, tracking and statements. | A B2B partner is a customer *of the tenant*, never a tenant itself. A partner user must only ever see the samples of their own organisation. |
| **B2C** (business-to-consumer) | A walk-in or home-collection patient who pays for themselves. | Default registration flow (C4); patient portal (C6); patient app, reminders, loyalty (C16, Phase 3). | The same patient can be B2C on one visit and arrive through a B2B partner on the next. Model the channel on the **order**, not on the patient. |
| **Cash vs credit** (payment mode at registration) | 00 A3: "Mark patient as OPD, IPD, cash or credit." *Cash* means the patient pays now (in any payment method: cash, card, wallet or QR). *Credit* means the amount is billed to a credit party later. | A field on the order (C4), separate from the visit type (OPD/IPD) and from the payment method recorded in the ledger. | "Cash" here is a payment *mode*, not the payment *method* "cash notes". The questionnaire lists OPD/IPD/cash/credit together, but they are two independent fields: an IPD patient can pay cash. |
| **Commission** | See **Referral incentive**. The word must never appear on any system document or UI label (04 L19). | — | — |
| **Credit party** | An organisation (company, hospital, insurer-like payer, B2B partner) that the lab bills on credit, typically with a monthly bill/statement (00 E6). | C3 master ("affiliations/credit parties"); C4 credit-party monthly billing and dues. Reports for credit-party orders are routinely released with dues outstanding (01 §1.4). | Not the patient. The order still has a patient; the payer is the credit party. Hold-till-paid logic must be able to treat credit-party dues differently (see **Hold-till-paid**, section E). |
| **Discount** | A reduction on one test line or on the whole bill. 00 A8 requires a reason and the identity of the approver. | C4 `frontdesk.discounts`: amount or percent, scope (line or bill), reason, approver, timestamp. Audited (04 D11). | A discount is not a price-list price. A doctor-specific price comes from the price list; a discount is an ad-hoc reduction on top of it. Never apply a discount by editing an issued invoice: invoices are immutable (04 L5). |
| **Due / balance due** | The unpaid amount on an order (billed minus paid). | `BillingPort.getOrderBalance`. The event `billing.balance.changed.v1` (C4 → C6, C16) drives the hold-till-paid UI hints. | Computed by `billing` only. Reporting and apps must ask the port; they must never compute dues themselves. |
| **IPD** (in-patient department) | A patient admitted to a hospital ward. A lab sees IPD orders when it serves a hospital, or when a hospital sends inpatient samples. | Visit type on the order (C4), with OPD as the other value. | Visit type, not payment mode (see **Cash vs credit**). |
| **OPD** (out-patient department) | A patient who is not admitted. In Nepali labs, most walk-ins are recorded as OPD. | Visit type on the order (C4). | Same as IPD. |
| **Order** (visit, registration, booking, requisition) | The visit-level request: one patient, one registration event, one set of order items (01 §2.1: "Order (visit-level) → OrderItem/Test → Specimen"). | `frontdesk.orders` (C4). Events: `frontdesk.order.created.v1`, `frontdesk.order.items_changed.v1`, `frontdesk.order.cancelled.v1`. A C16 booking becomes an order via `FrontdeskPort.createOrderFromBooking`. | An order is not an invoice: invoices live in `billing`, and cancelling an order makes a credit note there. An order is not a specimen (one order has many specimens). A patient-app booking is not yet an order. |
| **Order item** | One orderable placed on an order (one test, one panel or one package), with its price snapshot and priority. | `frontdesk.order_items` (C4). `CatalogPort.resolveOrderables` expands panels/packages into the tests that the lab must actually perform. | The order item is what was **sold**; the expanded tests are what is **performed**. Keep both. Billing lines follow order items; lab work and results follow the expanded tests. |
| **Orderable** | Anything that can be put on an order: a test, a panel/profile or a package. | Catalog concept (C3); resolved through `CatalogPort.resolveOrderables`. 07-BLOCKERS B7 makes tax rules data "per tenant, per orderable". | "Orderable" is a role, not a separate thing in the lab. Do not create results against a package; results attach to tests/analytes. |
| **Patient** | The person tested. Identified by demographics plus a lab-assigned identifier. 00 A2: search "by name, mobile number or lab code". | `frontdesk.patients` (C4), tenant-scoped. The same human at two tenants is two unrelated patient rows (tenant isolation, 04 D1/D2). | Do not make mobile number unique: families share phones (C16 supports family members under one login). Duplicate patient rows break previous results, trends and delta checks, so search-before-create is mandatory (01 §1.1). |
| **Price list** | A set of prices for orderables that applies to a doctor, hospital or collection centre (00 E3). It can be edited in Excel and uploaded back (00 E4). | C3: price lists per doctor/hospital/centre plus Excel import/export; `CatalogPort.priceFor`. The order item stores the price that was in effect at registration. | Never re-price historical order items when a price list changes. Membership/loyalty pricing (C16, Phase 3) is modelled as a price-list variant (01 §1.7). |
| **Referral incentive** (doctor commission, "cut") | Money a lab pays a referring doctor or centre based on referred business. It is common in the market but ethically and legally sensitive. NMC clause-level prohibition, NHPC codes and TDS treatment are **UNVERIFIED** (04 L19). | **DECISION (04 L19):** referral-incentive *reporting* only (C8). Never labelled "commission". Payouts happen outside the system or behind a tenant flag. BLOCKED B13 (client compliance position); until then, a tenant flag can hide the report entirely. | Do not build payout execution, and do not print "commission" anywhere. Referral attribution (who referred this order) is ordinary master data and is always kept. |
| **Referring doctor** (referrer, "Referred By") | The clinician who requested the tests. Printed on the report ("Referred By", 00 §4; "SELF" when the patient came without a referral). | Doctor master (C3) with quick-add at registration that is flagged for back-office verification (01 §1.1). Reports can be delivered to the referrer (00 D4). | "SELF" is a value meaning "no referrer"; it is not a doctor record. Quick-added doctors may be duplicates, so merging is a back-office task. |
| **Registration** (front-desk registration) | The front-desk step that creates or finds the patient, creates the order, applies pricing and discounts, takes payment, prints the receipt and barcode labels, and triggers the registration SMS. 00 A1 requires all of this on "one single page". | C4 owns the whole page (CM §5.1, "Ownership of the registration page"). `POST /v1/frontdesk/registrations`. | Registration is not accessioning: specimens are received in the lab later (section C). No other chunk adds UI to this page. |
| **Registration number** (Reg No, lab code, visit number) | The human-facing identifier of a registration/visit, printed on the report (Metro example "Reg No 0001EE000996", 00 §4). | **DECISION (G):** the registration number identifies the **order**. Its format is tenant-configurable (C2 "numbering prefixes"). The primary key is a separate UUIDv7 (04 D9). | Not the invoice number (an IRD fiscal-year series, section I). Not the specimen barcode. Never use it, or any sequential ID, in URLs or QR codes (04 D9). |
| **TRF** (test request form, requisition slip) | The doctor's paper or electronic request listing the tests. 00 A7: upload a photo or scan of it at registration. | `frontdesk` TRF attachments (C4), stored as private objects. | A TRF image contains PHI and must never go to a foreign service or into logs. It is an attachment, not structured order data: the operator still enters the order items. |
| **Urgent / STAT** | A priority marking that the lab must process an order or test faster. 00 A10: an extra charge plus an urgent mark for the lab. | Priority on the order/order item (carried in `frontdesk.order.created.v1`); urgent surcharge rules in C3; separate STAT TAT target per test (01 §2.7). | The surcharge is a billing line, and the priority is a workflow flag; keep both. "STAT" comes from Latin *statim*, "immediately". |

## B. Catalog (test master)

| Term | Definition | In our system | Don't confuse with / pitfalls |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Analyte** (parameter, component, observation) | The single measured or derived quantity that gets one result value, for example haemoglobin, WBC count, glucose or ALT. A report line in the Metro sample is one analyte (00 §4). | Catalog entity under a test (C3: "tests (analytes, units, methods, decimals, formulas, result types, workflow type)"). Results are stored per analyte. Instruments send their own analyte codes, which are mapped via `CatalogPort.mapInstrumentCode`. | Not the same as a test: CBC is one test with about 20 analytes (03 §1 assumption). Instrument analyte codes are vendor-specific: never store them as our analyte identity. |
| **Attachment-bearing test** | A test whose result includes one or more images or PDFs (HbA1c HPLC chromatograms, urine microscopy, allergy panels), with or without numeric fields (01 §3.5; 04 D14). | C3 result type; the C7 agent can watch a folder per instrument; C6 renders the attachments inline. | Do not promise to parse chromatogram PDFs automatically. The numeric value comes over the normal result channel or by manual entry; the picture is evidence (01 §3.5). |
| **Calculated parameter** (derived analyte, formula) | An analyte computed from other results rather than measured. Examples: LDL by Friedewald = TC − HDL − TG/5, valid only when TG < 400 mg/dL (**VERIFIED**, 01 log #9); eGFR; A/G ratio; NLR, PLR and Mentzer index (Metro "METHOD: Calculated", 00 §4). | Formulas are stored in the test master, not in code (01 §2.2). C5 evaluates them over sibling results from the same order. | Recompute when an input is amended. Honour validity conditions (Friedewald's TG limit). Never treat a missing input as 0. Units of the inputs must match the formula's assumed units. |
| **Critical limit** | The low/high thresholds that make a result a critical (panic) value. They are stored separately from reference ranges (01 §2.2). | C3 "critical limits"; `CatalogPort.criticalLimitsFor`. Detection and notification belong to C5 (see **Critical value**, section E). | A critical limit is not the edge of the reference range. A result can be "High" without being critical. |
| **Department** | The lab section that performs a test: haematology, biochemistry, immunology/serology, clinical pathology, microbiology, histopathology, radiology and so on (00 §4, 00 F2). | C3 master. Drives report grouping (C6), worklists (C5) and income-by-department MIS (C8). | Radiology is a department for billing and MIS, but its reports are narrative (section H). Department is not the same as the instrument or the branch. |
| **Method** | The analytical technique used to measure an analyte. Printed under every analyte line in the Metro report (e.g. "Fluorescence Flowcytometry", "Hexokinase", "Calculated", 00 §4). | C3 test-master attribute; a reference range can depend on the method. | The method is not the instrument. If a lab changes method, it usually needs new reference ranges, so version both together (04 D14). |
| **Package** (health package, health check-up) | A commercial bundle of tests/panels sold at one price, sometimes sex-specific (Metro "METRO HEALTH PACKAGE (ELITE) (MALE)", 00 §4). | **DECISION (G):** an orderable in C3 that expands, via `resolveOrderables`, into tests/panels. The report can group lines under the package name (00 §4). | Priced as a bundle: do not sum component prices. Results attach to the component tests, never to the package. |
| **Panel / profile** | A named, clinically meaningful group of tests ordered together (e.g. lipid profile, LFT, KFT). Labs use "panel" and "profile" interchangeably, and some call a multi-analyte test (CBC) a panel. | **DECISION (G):** "panel" = a C3 orderable grouping several **tests**, each of which may also be orderable alone. A single test with many analytes (CBC) is a **test**, not a panel. "Profile" is a synonym of panel. | 00 §4 calls CBC a "test panel" on the Metro report. That is report wording, not our data model. If C3's 05-SCHEMA models it differently, C3 wins via CR. |
| **Reference range** (biological reference interval, BRI, normal range, "Biological Ref Range") | The interval of values expected in a healthy reference population, often specific to age, sex and method. It is printed as a column on the report (00 §4). | C3 versioned reference ranges (04 D14, **DECISION**); `CatalogPort.referenceRangeFor`. Every result row stores a snapshot of the range used. The real ranges are BLOCKED B3; build against the synthetic catalog. | Never recompute flags on old results against a new range version. For ordinal results the "range" is an expected category (e.g. "Negative"). Not the same as critical limits, or the instrument's reportable range. |
| **Result type** | The shape of a result value: numeric, ordinal/semi-quantitative (urine nitrite 1+/2+), text/coded, or attachment (04 D14). | C3 test-master attribute; C5 validates entry by it. | Do not force every result into a decimal column: ordinal values must sort and compare by their defined order, not alphabetically. |
| **Short code** (canned comment, comment code) | An abbreviation that expands to a standard comment text, for example for RBC morphology (00 C3). Also used for test search ("CBC", 00 A6). | C3 short-code comment library; C5 expands it during result entry. | Two different short-code uses: comment short codes (result entry) and test short names (registration search). Store the expanded text with the result, so later edits to the library don't change old reports. |
| **Specimen type** (sample type) | The biological material a test needs: EDTA whole blood, serum, fluoride plasma ("FASTING PLASMA FL.", 00 §4), urine, stool, swab, tissue. Printed as the "Specimen:" line on the report. | C3 master. Links a test to an acceptable container type. | Specimen *type* is a catalog attribute; a *specimen* is a physical tube with a barcode (section C). |
| **TAT target** | The expected turnaround time for a test, with separate routine and STAT targets (01 §2.7). | C3 stores targets as data. Measurement is in C5/C8; alerts and escalations are in C9 (Phase 2). | A target is not a measurement. See **TAT** in section E for the start and stop points. |
| **Test** (investigation) | The catalog unit that the lab performs and reports, with one or more analytes, a specimen type, a department and a method. Single-analyte tests (glucose) have exactly one analyte. | C3 catalog entity; an orderable. `CatalogPort.getTest`. | Not an analyte (see Analyte). "Test" in everyday lab speech can mean a panel, a package or one line on the report: read the context and map it to our entities. |
| **Unit** | The unit of measure of a numeric result (g/dL, mmol/L, 10^3/µL, %). Printed in the Units column (00 §4). | C3, versioned with reference ranges (04 D14); C7 mappings can do unit conversion (01 §3.3). | The instrument's unit may differ from the reported unit, so conversion must be explicit in the mapping. Never compare values (delta checks, trends) across different units without converting. |
| **Workflow type** | A C3 test attribute (CM §2, C3 scope) that routes the test to the right processing path. | Its values are defined in C3's 05-SCHEMA. Glossary interpretation (confirm there): it separates routine results (C5), microbiology (C10), anatomic pathology (C11) and narrative reports (C6). | Do not hard-code routing by department name; use this attribute. |

## C. Specimens and collection

| Term | Definition | In our system | Don't confuse with / pitfalls |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Accession / accessioning** | The act of logging a specimen as received by the lab and giving it a lab identity. Some labs use "accession number" for the visit, others for the specimen, and histopathology uses it for the case. | **DECISION (G):** in our docs, accessioning = the specimen transition to `RECEIVED/ACCESSIONED` (01 §2.1). The visit identifier is the registration number (section A). The histology "accession" is a separate case number series (section H). | Registration (front desk) ≠ accessioning (lab receipt). Do not create a third, "accession number", identifier for routine specimens unless a CR adds it. |
| **Aliquot** | A portion of a specimen transferred into a child container, for example to split chemistry and immunoassay work from one primary tube, or to send part of a sample to an outside lab (01 §2.1). | Child specimen row in `lab` (C5), linked to its parent. It inherits the patient/order link, gets its own barcode, and tests can be assigned at aliquot level. | An aliquot is not a recollection (it comes from the same draw). Its barcode is new and must never equal the parent's. |
| **Barcode** (sample ID, barcode number, "Barcode No") | The machine-readable label on each container. It is generated automatically for each container at registration and printed as a sticker (00 B1; 01 §1.2). Metro example "E1100001677" (00 §4). | Specimen identifier in `lab` (C5). It is the key that analysers send back (host query `LabPort.getPendingTestsForBarcode`; result idempotency on `sample_barcode`, 04 N10). Default pattern `{prefix}{yy}{seq:8}` = prefix + 2-digit year + 8 digits, the client format (08 A13 D-8; 03-IC §9.3). | Not the registration number, not the UUID primary key. Must not repeat within a tenant: a recollection gets a new barcode and never reuses the rejected one (01 §2.1). It is printed in plain sight, so it must not encode PHI. |
| **Branch** | A physical site of the tenant's own lab business: a processing lab or a full service point. 00 G3 counts "branches and collection centres" together as 20. | `core` table `branches` (C0 DDL, C2 logic). Inventory stores exist per branch (C12). Staff branch scope is an explicit grant (`core.membership_branches`), used only for application-level checks on P0/P1 data (e.g. C12 stores); it never widens RLS (08 R3). | Not a tenant. Whether invoice numbering runs per branch is BLOCKED (04 L8); the default is per (tenant, fiscal year). |
| **Collection centre** (CC, sample collection centre) | A place where samples are collected and sent to the processing lab. It can be owned or franchised ("franchise based", 00 §3 item 1). It appears on the report as "Sample Collected At" with a code + name (00 §4). | `core.collection_centres`. Centre scoping is a second RLS predicate through a membership table (04 D2). Price lists can be per centre (00 E3), and reports can be delivered to the centre (00 D4). | Not a branch, not a tenant, not a B2B partner (though a franchised centre may also be a credit party). Metro uses a pseudo-centre "C000000002-HOME COLLECTION" (00 §4), so migrated data may show home collection as a centre. |
| **Container** (tube, vacutainer; tube colour) | The physical vessel and its additive (EDTA, clot activator/serum, fluoride, citrate, plain urine pot). Cap colour is a visual code for the additive. | C3 master "containers", linked to specimen types. One barcode label per container. | Colour conventions vary by manufacturer and are not verified in this research set. Store colour as tenant-editable display data. The identity is the additive/container type, never the colour. One container can serve many tests (a serum tube → about 20 chemistry tests, 01 §2.1). |
| **Home collection** | A phlebotomist collects the sample at the patient's home and brings it to the lab. | C17 (Phase 3; timing BLOCKED B1): offline-first Flutter app (04 S17), assigned visits, barcode scan at collection, payment captured as a ledger entry, sync via `homecollection.collection.synced.v1`. Bookings arrive via `engagement.booking.confirmed.v1`. | Logout is blocked while unsynced mutations exist (04 A15). An expired token gets no grace period: refresh, then upload (04 A7). |
| **Phlebotomy / phlebotomist** | Drawing blood (venipuncture); the person who does it. | Collection step in the specimen state machine (C5); phlebotomy app (C17). | Collection time (when the blood was drawn) is not receipt time (when the lab got it). Both are needed for TAT. |
| **Recollection** | Collecting a new specimen after the first one was rejected. | **New specimen row** linked to the rejected one; its tests re-point to the new specimen; new barcode (01 §2.1). Triggered by `lab.specimen.rejected.v1` (C5 → C4, C16, C17); C4 also flags it from `lab.specimen.status_changed.v1` with `to_status = recollection_ordered` (08 A13 D-6). Results are **new** `pending` rows; the old rows stay `cancelled` with `cancel_reason = 'specimen_rejected'` (08 A13 D-12). | Do not "un-reject" the old specimen or reuse its barcode. The rejection stays logged for QC indicators. |
| **Rejection** (specimen rejection) | The lab refuses a specimen as unfit for testing. It needs a coded reason plus free text. Example reasons: haemolysis, clot, QNS (quantity not sufficient), mislabel, wrong container, delayed transport (01 §2.1; 00 B2). | C5 transition from any state before `APPROVED` to `REJECTED`. It notifies the originating centre or phlebotomist and feeds the rejection-rate MIS. | Reasons form an extensible coded set, not free text only. Treating rejection rate as a standard ISO 15189 quality indicator is **UNVERIFIED** (04 L22). |
| **SIN** (sample identification number) | The term some Indian lab brands use for the sample barcode number (seen in the Healthians sample as "SIN/barcode", 00 §5). The expansion "sample identification number" is common usage, not verified in this research set. | No separate entity: it is the specimen **barcode** value. Our UI label follows the tenant (Metro prints "Barcode No"). | Not the registration number. |
| **Specimen** (sample) | One physical container of patient material with one barcode. One specimen can serve many tests, and one test can need several specimens (e.g. timed GTT draws) (01 §2.1). | `lab.specimens` (C5). State changes emit `lab.specimen.status_changed.v1`. | "Sample" also means a QC sample or an outside lab's sample. In our code, "specimen" always means a patient specimen. Specimen ≠ specimen type ≠ order. |
| **Specimen state machine** | The lifecycle of a specimen (01 §2.1): `ORDERED → COLLECTED → IN_TRANSIT → RECEIVED/ACCESSIONED → (ALIQUOTED) → IN_PROCESS → RESULTED → VERIFIED → APPROVED → REPORTED`. Any state before `APPROVED` can go to `REJECTED → RECOLLECTION_ORDERED`. Terminal extras: `STORED`, `DISCARDED`. | C5 owns it. Every transition is timestamped and user-stamped; TAT and the patient status board are derived from these timestamps. | The patient-facing status words (00 B3: "collected, in lab, ready, printed") are a projection of this machine, not separate states. Do not add states without a CR (other chunks consume the event). |
| **Transit** (scan-out / scan-in) | Movement of specimens from a collection centre to the processing lab: scanned out at the centre, scanned in at the hub (01 §2.1). | `IN_TRANSIT` state. The time between scan-out and scan-in is transport TAT, shown in B2B sample tracking (C13). | Transit is not receipt. A specimen in transit cannot be worked on. |

## D. Analytical workflow and instruments

| Term | Definition | In our system | Don't confuse with / pitfalls |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Analyser** (analyzer, instrument, machine) | A lab device that measures specimens and can send results electronically. The client's fleet is "Autobio, Mindray, Getein, etc."; the exact models are not given (00 G4, BLOCKED B2). | `instruments` schema (C7): instrument registry, mappings, message archive. A driver is written per model only once its official interface manual is obtained (07-BLOCKERS B2). | Analyser ≠ bridge agent (our software on a lab PC) ≠ device (the registered agent installation that holds a device token, 04 A13). Interface details for Autobio, Getein, urine and ID/AST systems are **UNVERIFIED** (04 N8). |
| **Analyte code mapping** | A translation table from an instrument's test/analyte codes to our analytes, including unit conversion and flag mapping (01 §3.3). | C7 mapping UI; `CatalogPort.mapInstrumentCode`. | It is per instrument model, and possibly per installed software version. An unmapped code must be held for review, never dropped or guessed. |
| **ASTM E1381 / E1394** (CLSI LIS1-A / LIS2-A2) | The classic lab-instrument protocol pair. E1381 / LIS1-A is the low-level framing over serial or TCP; E1394 / LIS2-A2 is the message content. LIS2-A2 is a revision of ASTM E1394-97 (**VERIFIED**, 04 N2). LIS1-A ↔ E1381 equivalence is **UNVERIFIED** (04 N3). | The bridge agent supports ASTM and HL7 v2 from sprint 1 (04 N1). The framing layer is written in-house (04 N13). | ASTM is a protocol family, not a vendor. Do not assume a given analyser speaks ASTM: Mindray manuals seen so far are HL7 (04 N6/N7, **UNVERIFIED** copies). |
| **ASTM record** | A line-level record inside an ASTM message. Types: H (header), P (patient), O (order), R (result), C (comment), Q (request information / query), L (terminator), S (scientific), M (manufacturer) (**VERIFIED**, 04 N5). | Parsed by the C7 driver framework. | **Q is the host-query record, not "QC".** How a driver marks a QC result (control sample ID or similar) is model-specific and **UNVERIFIED** (01 §2.5). |
| **Batch** | **DECISION (G):** a user-defined group of specimens processed together outside the instrument-run concept, such as a molecular PCR plate or extraction batch (01 §1.7, "batch/run concept"). Labs use "batch" and "run" loosely and sometimes interchangeably. | Specified by C11 (molecular) and C5 (worksheets) schemas. | Not a QC run (see **Run**). Do not apply Westgard "within-run" logic to a batch unless the batch is also the QC-bounded run. |
| **Bi-directional interface** | The analyser both sends results to the LIS **and** receives test orders from it (01 §3.1). Two styles: (1) **host query**: the instrument reads the barcode and asks the LIS; (2) **worklist/batch download**: the LIS pushes pending orders ahead of time. | **DECISION (04 N14):** upload results for every interfaced instrument; host query only where the vendor manual confirms it; contracts carry a "pending vendor documentation" clause per instrument. | The client asked for "7 instruments bi-directional" (00 §3 item 3). Do not promise it per instrument without the manual (B2). Haematology and POCT devices are often upload-only (01 §1.7, §3.2). |
| **HL7 v2** | A messaging standard widely used by newer analysers and middleware, commonly v2.3.1–2.5.1 (01 §3.1). Message set we implement: ORU^R01 in; ORM^O01 / OML^O21 out; inbound host queries per vendor; QBP where a vendor uses it; ACK original (AA/AE/AR) and enhanced (CA/CE/CR) (04 N9, **VERIFIED** specs / **DECISION**). | C7 parser framework; carried over MLLP (section K). Vendor-specific host-query pairs: Mindray BC-5380 ORM^O01 → ORR^O02; BS-120/200 QRY^Q02 → QCK^Q02 + DSR^Q03 (**UNVERIFIED**, unofficial copies only, 04 N6/N7). | HL7 v2 ≠ FHIR. "HL7" from an instrument vendor almost always means v2 with vendor-specific segments. Do not hard-code one vendor's query pattern as the generic one. |
| **Host query** (query mode) | The instrument scans a tube barcode and asks the LIS which tests to run on it. The LIS answers within about seconds (01 §3.1). | `LabPort.getPendingTestsForBarcode` (C5 implements; C7 calls). It is a capability flag per instrument (07-BLOCKERS B2). | The reply must contain only tests that are pending for that barcode on that instrument. Returning tests already resulted causes duplicate runs. Not the same as a worklist download. |
| **Instrument result time vs received_at** | Every analyser result carries the instrument's own timestamp; the server adds `received_at` (04 N12). | Store both. Display server time. Track clock offset per site (04 N12). The instrument time is part of the result-level idempotency key (04 N10). | Lab PC and analyser clocks drift. Never order results or compute TAT from instrument time alone. |
| **LIS vs LIMS** | Industry usage varies. *LIS* (laboratory information system) traditionally means a patient-centric clinical lab system; *LIMS* (laboratory information management system) often means a sample-centric research/industrial system. In instrument standards (CLSI LIS1/LIS2), "LIS" means whatever host computer talks to the analyser. | **DECISION (G):** our product is "LIMS" (00 header), and we treat LIS and LIMS as synonyms for it. In interface documents, "LIS" = our server side (ingest API + `LabPort`) as seen by the instrument. | Do not design two systems because both words appear in the docs. |
| **Middleware** | Vendor or third-party software sitting between instruments and the LIS (e.g. for rules or reagent telemetry). | Not used by default. Our bridge agent plays the connectivity role. Mirth Connect is a design reference only (4.6+ commercial; 4.5.2 last open-source, 04 T6). Reagent telemetry from middleware is not assumed (04 N15). | Our bridge agent is not middleware in the vendor sense: it does no clinical rules. |
| **Run** (analytical run) | **DECISION (G):** a period of patient testing on one instrument for one analyte, bounded by QC events. It is the unit that Westgard "within-run" rules evaluate and that "hold results since last good QC" refers to. | C9 QC run acceptance/rejection; event `quality.qc_run.rejected.v1`. | Not a batch, not a worksheet. A run belongs to one instrument; never mix instruments in one run. |
| **Uni-directional interface** | Results upload only. The operator programs tests at the instrument keypad (01 §3.1). | The default for any instrument without a confirmed host-query manual (04 N14). Results are matched by barcode. | Barcode matching still applies. Without host query, a keypad typing error can attach results to the wrong test, so unmatched results must queue for review. |
| **Worklist** | The live queue of received, unresulted specimens/tests for a department or analyser (01 §2.2). For bi-directional instruments, the LIS is the worklist source. | C5 worklists by department/analyser; pending list. | A worklist is a live query, not a document. Not a worksheet (below). |
| **Worksheet** | A printable or assigned list of pending tests for technicians (00 F4: "print worksheets for technicians"). In some systems (e.g. SENAITE) it also carries QC positions and instrument mapping (01 §1.2). | **DECISION (G):** a C5 artefact generated from the worklist at a point in time and assigned to a bench or person. | It is a snapshot. Results are still entered against tests/specimens, never "into the worksheet" as a separate store. |

| Term | Definition | In our system | Don't confuse with / pitfalls |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Addendum** | Supplementary anatomic report that adds information without correcting the original, e.g. an IHC added on an existing block (01 §2.4). | **DECISION (G):** used only for anatomic-pathology add-on sections (C11). C11 → C5 amendment with reason `supplementary_report`; report kind `addendum` (08 R1). Any change to an approved routine result is an **amended report**. | Not an amendment (which corrects). If the original text changes, it is an amendment and needs a new version. |
| **Amended report** (corrected report, revised report) | A new version of a report issued after approval because a result changed. No other chunk talks to SMS/WhatsApp/email providers directly. |
| **Delta check** | Compares a new result with the same patient's previous value for the same analyte within a time window, and flags it if the absolute or percentage change exceeds the analyte's threshold (01 §2.2). | **Flag** (H, L, HH, LL, abnormal flag) | A marker on a result: H/L = above/below the reference range; critical flags (often written HH/LL) = beyond critical limits (01 §2.2). Instrument flags (e.g. sample error) are not range flags. Ordinal results flag by category, not by number. |
| **Hold-till-paid** (hold until dues paid, release gate) | The report is not released to the patient until the balance due is paid (00 D1). | C6 release gate that asks `BillingPort.getReleaseEligibility(orderId)`. Reporting must not compute dues itself. Not the same as a QC hold (section F). |
| **ISO 15189** | The international standard for medical laboratory quality and competence. The verified obligation in this research set is the critical-result notification protocol (04 L21). That is not ISO 15189 accreditation. |
| **Lock** (result lock) | After approval, a result cannot be edited (00 C6; 04 R1). | DB and API enforce it in C5: no UPDATE path on approved values; changes only via amendment. | A UI-only lock is not a lock. The API must reject edits too. |
| **Narrative report** | A free-text report with headings and optional images, used for radiology, USG, endoscopy and cytogenetics (00 D9; 04 §12). | C6 narrative report engine (Phase 1), shared with histopathology templates. PACS/DICOM is out of scope (04 §12). | Not a structured result: it has no reference ranges or flags. Still versioned and still approved/locked like any report. |
| **Outsourced test** (send-out, referral test) | A test performed by another lab. Its PDF report is combined into ours (00 D5). | Aliquot for send-out (01 §2.1); the outside PDF is stored against the order and merged at render time. **DECISION (04 R7):** outside PDFs are untrusted: malware scan, sandboxed qpdf merge, never through the Chromium pool. | Outsourced ≠ "outsourced to us" (B2B partner samples coming in). The outside lab's PDF is not our approved result, so do not create our result rows from it unless they are entered and verified. |
| **Preliminary vs final report** (and partial report) | *Preliminary*: issued before results are complete or final (typical in microbiology: Gram stain → growth → final with AST, 01 §2.3). *Final*: complete and approved (Metro "Report Status: Final", 00 §4). *Partial*: some tests on the order are final while others are pending (`lab.order.ready_for_report.v1` covers "the releasable subset", CM §5.1). | C6 report versions with a status label; C10 micro prelim/final (Phase 2). | Preliminary describes the *result's* maturity; partial describes the *order's* completeness. A final report can be followed by an amended one. Label each PDF clearly. |
| **Previous results** (cumulative results, history, trend graph) | The patient's earlier results for the same analytes, shown beside the entry grid (00 C2) and as a graph (00 C7). Compare only like units and methods, or show the change visibly. |
| **QR verification** | A QR code on the report that opens a public page confirming the report is genuine (00 D6). | **DECISION (04 R3, A11):** ≥128-bit CSPRNG random token, server-stored. The page shows only: lab name; patient initials or masked name; test panel name(s); issue date/time + version; status valid/superseded. Never results, phone, address or DOB. Revoked and unknown tokens get an identical response. Rate-limited (04 §4). | Not a signed/HMAC URL. Not a report link (which needs an OTP and shows results). Never use report or registration numbers in the QR. |
| **Release** (report release, bulk release) | Making an approved report version available to its recipients (patient, referring doctor, collection centre). Bulk release = many reports at once (00 D8). | C6; event `reporting.report.released.v1` (→ C8, C13, C16). Release rules: hold-till-paid via the billing port, partial release, bulk release. | Release ≠ approval ≠ delivery. Approval locks the result, release publishes a report version, delivery sends notifications about it. Each step has its own timestamp. |
| **Report version** | An immutable rendered report (PDF) for an order at a point in time. | `reporting` reports + versions. A reprint of the same version is an access event (`access_log`, 04 D11), not a new version. |
| **Result** | The value recorded for one analyte on one specimen/test, with its origin (instrument or manual), flags, range snapshot and verification state. | `lab.results` (C5), with verifications and amendments. Instrument results arrive via `instruments.result.received.v1` → `LabPort.ingestInstrumentResult`. | Record the origin (auto vs manual, 01 §2.2). The instrument value as received must be kept even when a technologist reruns or edits before verification (audit, 04 D11). |
| **Signatory** (authorised signatory) | The pathologist(s) whose name, qualification, NMC registration number and signature image appear in the report footer (00 §4). | C6 template/letterhead system (signatures, logo, watermark, letterhead on/off). | The printed signature represents an approval that happened in the system. The signatory shown should be the approving user, or the tenant's nominated signatory per its template rules (C6 PRD). Never let the template invent an approval. |
| **Smart report** | A patient-friendly summary layer built from results: health score, organ tiles, deranged-parameter summary, advice, follow-up test suggestions (00 §5; 01 §5). | C14, rules-based with a pathologist-approved template library (04 R8). Supplement upsell is excluded. An optional LLM must be self-hosted in Nepal; a foreign API needs founder approval. | Not the clinical report: it never replaces the approved results pages. Every text fragment comes from approved templates; nothing is generated freely. |

## F. Quality control (QC)

| Term | Definition | In our system | Don't confuse with / pitfalls |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Classic vs modern multirule** | Westgard's two ways of applying the rules. *Classic*: 4-1s and 10x are evaluated only after a 1-2s warning has fired. *Modern*: they are evaluated independently (01 §2.5). | **Per-lab configuration flag** (04 L23). Its default must be set explicitly in C9 config. | Do not silently pick one. The same data can pass under classic and fail under modern. |
| **Control level** | One concentration of a control material (e.g. normal, abnormal-high). Usually 2–3 levels per analyte (01 §2.5). | C9 setup; each level has its own target mean and SD. N per run counts control measurements across levels (04 L23). | Rules like 2-2s may compare across levels within a run. Do not compute one mean over different levels. |
| **Control material** (QC material, control) | A stable material with known expected values, run on the instrument like a patient sample to check that the analytical process works. | C9 "QC material/lot/target setup". QC results arrive via `instruments.qc.received.v1` (C7 → C5 capture, C9). | A control is not a calibrator: calibrators set the instrument's scale, controls check it. Inventory deducts both, by different models (01 §2.6). |
| **Corrective action** | What the technologist did after a QC failure (recalibrate, new control vial, maintenance) before rerunning QC (01 §2.5). | C9 corrective-action log, required before the run is released. | Accreditation assessors ask for it (01 §2.5). A rerun with no recorded action is incomplete. |
| **In control / out of control** | A run is *in control* when no rejection rule fires, and *out of control* when one does (01 §2.5). | C9 run status. | A 1-2s warning alone does not make a run out of control. |
| **Levey-Jennings chart** (LJ chart, LJ curve) | A plot of QC results over time/run for one analyte × instrument × control level, with horizontal lines at mean, ±1SD, ±2SD, ±3SD (**VERIFIED**, 01 §2.5). | C9 (Phase 2): "real-time LJ curve" per analyser (00 §3 item 16). | One chart per analyte × instrument × level (and lot). Do not merge levels or lots on one line. |
| **Lot** | A manufacturing batch of control material (or reagent). It carries its own expiry and, for controls, its own expected values. | C9 material/lot/target setup; reagent lots in C12 (lots/expiry, FEFO). | Setting new targets when a control lot changes is standard practice, not a 04 fact: let C9 specify it. A reagent lot is not a control lot. |
| **N** (controls per run) | The number of control measurements in a run. It determines which Westgard rule set applies (04 L23). | The C9 engine reads N from the QC configuration (04 L23). | See the N-dependent rules below. Do not hard-code the N=2 rule set. |
| **QC data capture** (Phase 1) | Storing QC results from analysers before any rule engine exists. | C5 captures QC raw data from day one (CM §2; 04 §12); the C9 engine follows in Phase 2. | In Phase 1 nothing auto-rejects or auto-holds. Do not imply QC enforcement in the UI. |
| **Run rejection** (QC failure, hold since last good QC) | When a rejection rule fires: mark the run out of control, hold patient results for that analyte since the last in-control QC, require a corrective action, rerun QC, then release (01 §2.5). | C9 emits `quality.qc_run.rejected.v1` → C5 calls `LabPort.holdResultsSince`. Auto-verification is blocked (`QualityPort.qcStatusFor`). | A QC hold blocks verification/release of the affected results. It is unrelated to hold-till-paid. "Since last good QC" means by run time on that instrument and analyte, not "today". |
| **Target mean / SD** | The expected average and standard deviation of a control level, used to draw LJ lines and evaluate rules. Established from baseline runs; the "≥20 runs" minimum is common practice but **UNVERIFIED** (01 §2.5). | C9 setup per analyte × instrument × level (and lot). | Not the manufacturer's insert range unless the lab chooses so. Changing targets must be versioned, because old points are evaluated against the targets in force at the time. |
| **Westgard multirule** (Westgard rules) | A set of statistical decision rules applied to QC results to accept or reject a run. Definitions below are from 04 L23 (**VERIFIED**, westgard.com) and 01 §2.5. The error-type mapping comes from a secondary source (01 §2.5). | C9 engine (Phase 2), per lab classic/modern, N-aware. | Implement each rule exactly as defined. Do not invent a generic "range" rule. |
| **Westgard rule 1-2s** | One control observation exceeds mean ± 2SD. **Warning** only: it triggers inspection by the rejection rules and does not reject on its own (04 L23). | C9. In classic mode it gates 4-1s and 10x. | Not a rejection. |
| **Westgard rule 1-3s** | One control observation exceeds mean + 3SD or mean − 3SD. **Rejection**; indicates random error (01 §2.5). | C9. | — |
| **Westgard rule 10x** | Ten consecutive observations on the same side of the mean. **Rejection**; systematic shift; "usually has to be applied across runs and often across materials" (01 §2.5). Part of the N = 2/4 rule set (04 L23). | C9. Needs history across runs and levels. | Not part of the N = 3 set, which uses 6x/9x (04 L23). |
| **Westgard rule 2-2s** | Two consecutive control observations exceed the **same** +2SD or the same −2SD limit. **Rejection**; systematic shift. Applying it across levels within one run and for the same level across runs is common practice; the letter of the rule on this is **UNVERIFIED** (01 §2.5). Part of the N = 2/4 set (04 L23). | C9. | One above +2SD and one below −2SD is **not** 2-2s (that is R-4s). |
| **Westgard rule 2of3 2s** | Part of the N = 3 rule set (04 L23). By name: two of three control observations exceed the same ±2SD limit. 04/01 do not spell out its exact definition; the C9 TRD §7.5 definition is **DECISION (by analogy with 2-2s/4-1s/10x; UNVERIFIED as to the letter)**, pinned by golden vectors (08 A17 D-58). | C9, N = 3 configuration only. | Use the C9 TRD §7.5 definition (D-58); research holds no exact text for it, and no web source is added. |
| **Westgard rule 3-1s** | Part of the N = 3 rule set (04 L23). By name: three consecutive observations exceed the same ±1SD limit. 04/01 do not spell out its exact definition; C9 TRD §7.5 = **DECISION / UNVERIFIED as to the letter** (08 A17 D-58). | C9, N = 3 only. | Same caveat as 2of3 2s. |
| **Westgard rule 4-1s** | Four consecutive observations exceed the same +1SD or the same −1SD limit. **Rejection**; systematic shift/trend; often applied across runs and materials (01 §2.5). Part of the N = 2/4 set (04 L23). | C9. In classic mode it is evaluated only after a 1-2s warning. | Not in the N = 3 set (3-1s there). |
| **Westgard rules 6x / 8x / 9x / 12x** | "n consecutive observations on the same side of the mean" variants of 10x. 8x/10x/12x belong to the N = 2/4 set; 6x/9x to the N = 3 set (04 L23). | C9, chosen by N. | 10x is VERIFIED (04 L23, 01 §2.5). 6x, 8x, 9x and 12x are named in 04 L23 but research holds no exact text for them: their C9 TRD §7.5 definitions are **DECISION (by analogy with 10x; UNVERIFIED as to the letter)**, pinned by golden vectors (08 A17 D-58). Which variants apply is set by N (04 L23), not hard-coded. |
| **Westgard rule R-4s** | One control measurement in a group exceeds mean **+2SD** and another exceeds mean **−2SD**. **Within-run only**. **Rejection**; random error/imprecision (04 L23; 01 §2.5). | C9; evaluated only inside one run. | **Not "range > 4SD".** +3.5SD and −0.6SD has a range > 4SD but is not R-4s (it is caught by 1-3s). Never evaluate between runs. |

| Term | Definition | In our system | Don't confuse with / pitfalls |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Antibiogram** (cumulative antibiogram) | A periodic summary of organism × antibiotic susceptibility rates (%S per year) across the lab's isolates (01 §2.3). | Export from C10 data (MIS). | Built from interpretations, so the standard and version (CLSI/EUCAST) must be consistent within one antibiogram. |

| **EUCAST** | European Committee on Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing. Categories S, I = "susceptible, increased exposure", R (04 D14). | Interpretive standard code "EUCAST" + version (04 D14). | See CLSI vs EUCAST. |

| **Organism** | The identified micro-organism (species or group), coded from a SNOMED-CT-derived list or a curated local list (01 §2.3). | C10 coded list. | SNOMED CT licensing for Nepal is **UNVERIFIED** (01 §2.4); use a local list by default. |

| **Reconciliation** | Comparing our ledger/intents with the gateway's records to catch missed callbacks, gateway-side refunds and fraud. | **DECISION (04 P7):** daily automated job (C15 worker) using eSewa status check and Khalti lookup; Fonepay mechanism **UNVERIFIED**; static QR reconciled manually from the bank statement. Pending/AMBIGUOUS states are re-polled; discrepancies alert finance (REQ-SEC-054). | Reconciliation never silently edits the ledger; it creates findings and, where approved, reversing entries. |
| **Refund / reversal** | Returning money (refund) or cancelling a recorded payment (reversal). | C15 refunds workflow; the ledger records it as a reversing row (04 P1). Desk refunds (C4) need two people: `requestRefund` (`billing.refund.request`) then `approveRefund` (`billing.refund.approve`); approver = requester → `billing.refund_self_approval_forbidden` (08 R15, A13 D-24). Gateway states `Refunded`/`Partially Refunded` (Khalti) and FULL_REFUND/PARTIAL_REFUND (eSewa) are hold + escalate (04 P4). | A refund is not a credit note. If the invoice is also cancelled, that is a separate billing action. |
| **transaction_uuid** | eSewa's merchant-generated unique transaction ID: alphanumeric and hyphen (04 P3). | Generated by C15 per intent (a UUID string is valid). | It is one of the signed fields; changing it after signing breaks verification. |

## K. Platform and program terms

| Term | Definition | In our system | Don't confuse with / pitfalls |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ACK** (acknowledgement, HL7 and ASTM) | HL7: an ACK message with codes AA/AE/AR (original) or CA/CE/CR (enhanced) (04 N9). ASTM low level: ACK/NAK control characters per frame (04 N4). | C7 bridge agent. The raw frame is persisted to the local encrypted SQLite **before** ACK (04 N11). | Never ACK before persisting: a crash would lose a result the instrument thinks was delivered. |
| **ASTM frame** (low-level transfer) | E1381 framing: `ENQ`/`ACK`/`NAK`/`EOT` handshake; frames `STX FN text ETB-or-ETX C1 C2 CR LF`; FN = frame number 1–7 then 0; checksum = sum of FN..ETB/ETX mod 256 as 2 uppercase hex characters; max frame 247 characters (240 data); contention: instrument retries after 1 s, host defers 20 s (**VERIFIED**, Beckman Coulter LIS1-A implementation, 04 N4). | In-house framing layer in the Go agent (04 N13). | Messages longer than 240 data characters span several frames (ETB intermediate, ETX last). Reassemble before parsing records. |
| **audit_log / access_log** | (a) `audit_log`: trigger-based JSONB old/new rows with tenant, centre, actor, IP, user agent, request id, time. (b) `access_log`: app-level view/print/download/export/consent/role events. Both append-only (no UPDATE/DELETE grants), shipped to a separate store, retained ≥5 years (04 D11). | `audit` schema (C0 tables/triggers, C2 viewer). | Do not put PHI values into application logs to "help debugging". The audit store is the controlled place for change history. |
| **BFF** (backend-for-frontend) | A server layer that owns the browser session and proxies API calls for one UI. | **DECISION (04 S4):** the staff Next.js app is a server-only BFF. No browser-side Supabase client holding a session; HttpOnly + Secure + SameSite=Lax cookies set by server code; live updates via SSE/polling through the server. Idle 15–30 min, absolute ≤12 h (04 A8). | The BFF does not hold business logic: the Fastify API is the only write path (04 S2). |
| **Blocker (B#)** | An item that cannot be finalised until an outside party answers (04 §13). | `program/07-BLOCKERS.md` gives the default to build against. | A blocker never stops a chunk. Never present a default as the real answer in UI text, invoices or anything shown to IRD (07-BLOCKERS). |
| **Bridge agent** | Our on-premises service on a lab PC that talks to analysers over serial/TCP, parses ASTM/HL7, buffers results and uploads them to the cloud. | **DECISION (04 S16, N11):** Go static binary as a Windows service; Windows 10/11 or Server 2016+ only; toolchain Go ≥1.26; encrypted local SQLite queue; at-least-once batched upload with backoff; heartbeat every 60 s; signed config + signed self-update; outbound-only HTTPS; low-privilege account. C7 owns `apps/bridge-agent`. | Not middleware with clinical rules. A Linux build is deferred. The agent's local queue is truncated after upload (e.g. 72 h bound); the ≥1-year raw archive is **server-side** (04 I8). |
| **BYPASSRLS** | A Postgres role attribute that ignores RLS policies. Supabase's `postgres`, `service_role`, `supabase_admin`, `supabase_etl_admin` and `supabase_read_only_user` have it (**VERIFIED**, 04 D5). | No request path uses those roles. Tenant tables are owned by a NOLOGIN, non-BYPASSRLS owner role; CI asserts `rolbypassrls = false` for every app role (04 D5). | FORCE RLS alone does not stop a BYPASSRLS role. |
| **Change request (CR)** | The formal request to change something a chunk doesn't own: DDL, contracts, shared packages, core, or another chunk's port/event (`04-CHANGE-REQUESTS.md`). | Filed as an issue `CR-<from>-<nnn>`; additive changes approved by the owner; breaking changes ship as a new version (`.v2`, `/v2`). | Never work around a missing contract (SQL into another schema, undeclared fields, copied components). Keep working against the fake. |
| **Chunk** | A unit of parallel work with exclusively owned paths: C0–C18 (CM §2). | CM §2 list; each chunk has the seven documents in `docs/chunks/Cxx-*/`. | A chunk is not a microservice: most chunks are modules inside the one API, worker and web app. |
| **Coded set** | A list of allowed codes kept as data, extensible without code changes (e.g. AST interpretive categories, rejection reasons). | **DECISION (04 D14)** for AST; the same pattern for other clinical code lists. Values are added through C0 DDL/seed via CR. | Not a hard-coded TS/SQL enum. 04 explicitly forbids a 3-value S/I/R enum. |
| **Composite foreign key** | FKs on `(tenant_id, id)` with parents `UNIQUE (tenant_id, id)` (04 D8). | All tenant tables (C0 DDL). | Makes cross-tenant references impossible at the DB level, even through bugs. Do not add single-column FKs to tenant tables. |
| **Contract** | A shared shape written once by C0: DDL + RLS, OpenAPI per domain, event JSON Schemas, port interfaces, error codes, routes, design tokens (CM §0, §3 `contracts/`). | `contracts/`, `db/` (C0 only). | Chunk docs (`05-SCHEMA.md`, API sections) are the *specification*; C0's files are the *contract*. Code consumes the contract. |
| **Device token** (bridge device token) | The credential a bridge agent installation uses to call the ingest API. | **DECISION (04 A13):** ≥256-bit CSPRNG, shown once, stored as SHA-256 hash, one per device per centre, individually revocable; DPAPI-encrypted on the agent. Ingest limit: 60 requests/min/device; batches ≤100 messages/request (04 §4). | Not a user JWT. Never log it. Revoking one device must not affect others. |
| **Event** | An asynchronous fact that something happened, published by its owning module (CM §5 R4). | Past tense, versioned (`frontdesk.order.created.v1`), envelope `event_id` (UUIDv7), `tenant_id`, `occurred_at`, `actor`, `idempotency_key`; JSON Schemas in `contracts/events/`; delivered as Graphile Worker jobs enqueued in the same transaction (outbox). | Consumers must be idempotent on `event_id`. Events are not commands: "please create specimens" is wrong, "order created" is right. |
| **Fake** | A stand-in implementation of a port or API that behaves per contract, so chunks can build before the real owner delivers. | `packages/fakes` (C0), with shared contract tests in `packages/fakes/contract-tests/`; Prism + MSW mocks for HTTP (04 S19). Registered until the owner merges (CM §5 R5). | A fake that passes tests the real implementation fails is a bug in the fake. Both must pass the same contract suite (CM §8 risk 3). |
| **Fastify API** (only write path) | The Node.js API server. It is the **only write path** for clinical/billing data (04 S2). | `services/api` (C0 core + chunk modules under `src/modules/<module>`). | Supabase PostgREST, if used at all, exposes only curated low-risk read views (04 S3). |
| **Foreign touchpoint** | Any service outside Nepal that receives our data. | Register in 04 §11: email relay (pre-allowed pattern, link-only); WhatsApp (link-only); SMS gateways (treated as cross-border); HIBP, external LLM, foreign CDN only with founder approval (B11). | Do not add one without founder approval (CM §4 "may not"). No PHI through any of them. |
| **Graphile Worker** | The Postgres-backed job queue. | **DECISION (04 S10):** transactional enqueue (= outbox) + cron; BullMQ rejected; Redis only as an ephemeral rate-limit cache (04 S11). Least-privilege operation (pre-installed schema, no DDL rights) is **UNVERIFIED** (04 D6). | Jobs must set tenant context per job (transaction-local) before touching tenant tables. |
| **Idempotency (ingest)** | Guarantees that an analyser result re-sent by the agent is stored once. | **DECISION (04 N10):** message level: (instrument_id, MSH-10) for HL7, (instrument_id, sha256(raw)) for ASTM. Result level: unique (instrument_id, sample_barcode, analyte_code, instrument_result_time). | Both levels are required: message-level alone misses the same result inside a differently framed message. |
| **MLLP** (Minimal Lower Layer Protocol) | The TCP framing for HL7 v2: start byte 0x0B, message, end bytes 0x1C 0x0D (04 N9). | C7 bridge agent. | Without MLLP framing, TCP stream boundaries do not equal message boundaries. Buffer until the end bytes. |
| **MSH-10** | The HL7 message control ID field in the MSH (header) segment. | Message-level idempotency key for HL7 together with instrument_id (04 N10); echoed in the ACK. | Some instruments reuse control IDs after a restart. That is why result-level uniqueness also exists. |
| **Outbox** (transactional outbox) | The pattern of committing a domain change and the job that performs its side-effect in the **same transaction**, so neither happens without the other. | **DECISION (03 §4.2; 04 S10):** no external side-effect (SMS/WhatsApp/email/CBMS/webhook) from request handlers. Handler commits + `add_job`; the worker performs it with retries and an idempotency key and records the delivery status. | Calling an SMS API inside an HTTP handler is a defect even if it "works". |
| **Owned path** | A repo path that belongs to exactly one chunk (CM §3). | CODEOWNERS + CI path check reject PRs touching unowned paths. | `db/`, `contracts/`, `packages/*` and every `core/` folder are C0-only. |
| **Phase** | Product release phase from 04 §12: 1, 1.5–2, 2, 3, DROP. | Phase 1 = Wave 1, Phase 2 = Wave 2, Phase 3 = Wave 3 (CM §1). A requirement applies from the phase its component ships (04 §12 rule). | Phase is about the product; wave is about build dependencies. |
| **Phase stub** | Minimal deny/disable implementation of a port whose owner ships in a later phase. | 02-CV §8.2 exception; 08 R9. | Not a fake (no data, no seeding). |
| **PHI** (protected health information) | Any information about a patient's health linked to an identifiable person: results, reports, TRFs, demographics tied to tests. | Stays on Nepal servers (04 S1). Never in fixtures, logs, tickets or AI prompts (`00-README.md` hard rule 2). Never in SMS/WhatsApp/email bodies (04 R4–R6). | A phone number plus "your report is ready" is still sensitive. Keep message bodies generic and link-only. |
| **Port** | A synchronous, in-process TypeScript interface that one module implements for others within a request (CM §5). | `contracts/ports/*.ts`. The owner implements; consumers depend on the interface only; the fake is registered until the owner merges (R5). Example: `BillingPort.getReleaseEligibility`. | Not an HTTP call and not an event. Never bypass a port by reading the owner's tables (R1). |
| **Published view** | A read-only view `<schema>.v_pub_*` with read grants, for cross-module **reporting** reads only (CM §5). | Defined in C0 DDL, specified in the owner's 05-SCHEMA. Example: `billing.v_pub_invoice_daily`. Consumers: C8 analytics, C13/C16 read models. | Not a write channel. Views use `security_invoker = true` (04 D4) so RLS still applies. |
| **QR verify token** | See **QR verification** (section E). | 04 A11, R3. | — |
| **RLS** (row-level security) | Postgres policies that filter rows per user/tenant. | **DECISION (04 D2/D4):** ENABLE + FORCE RLS on every tenant table; explicit per-command policies `TO lims_app` (group role of the API and worker logins; `authenticated` only on Supabase-served objects — 03-IC X9); predicate `tenant_id = (SELECT app.current_tenant_id())`; no `USING (true)`; CI checks; collection-centre scoping as a second predicate via membership. Every endpoint and table gets a cross-tenant probe test (`00-README.md` hard rule 5). Non-staff actors (partner/portal/patient) get no centre scope and are excluded from `__tenant` policies by `app.is_staff_or_system()` unless a table has an actor-specific policy (08 R3/R10). | Whether self-hosted Storage/Realtime populate `request.jwt.claims` the same way is **UNVERIFIED** (04 D3), so prove it with probes. |
| **Session kind** | Type of login session. | `tenant_staff`, `platform`, `partner`, `patient`, `staff_mobile` (C2; 08 R10). | Not an actor type (`user`, `system`, `device`, `portal`, `patient`, `partner`). |
| **Supabase (self-hosted)** / **GoTrue** | The self-hosted Supabase stack: GoTrue (Auth), Storage, Realtime (04 S2). | Used for auth, storage and Flutter Realtime only, and kept removable (04 S2). Realtime only for Flutter apps, private channels only (04 S5). GoTrue password hashing is bcrypt at fixed cost 10 (04 A2). | Gateway, Studio and pg-meta are never public. The stock compose binds ports on 0.0.0.0, so it must be overridden (04 S6). |
| **Synthetic data** | Generated, fake patient and test data. | `packages/test-fixtures` (C0); seeds in `db/seeds` are synthetic only. The real test list is BLOCKED B3. | The client's real report PDFs are confidential: never commit them and never paste them into AI tools (00 §7.5). |
| **Tenant** | One lab business that subscribes to the platform: the IRD taxpayer whose billing data must be stored separately (04 L4). Metro Labs is the anchor tenant (00). | `core.tenants`; `tenant_id uuid NOT NULL` on every tenant table, no default, set via claim/trigger (04 D1). | Tenant ≠ branch ≠ collection centre ≠ B2B partner. Whether one tenant may hold several PANs/legal entities is not addressed in 04, so raise a CR rather than assume. |
| **Tenant context** | The tenant (and actor) of the current request or job, as seen by Postgres. | The API verifies the JWT itself and sets `request.jwt.claims` with `set_config(..., true)` / `SET LOCAL`, transaction-local only; bare `SET` is banned (04 S9, D2). | **Never** take tenant_id from the body, a header or a query parameter (04 D2). With transaction pooling (PgBouncer), a session-level SET would leak to the next request. |
| **UUIDv7** | Time-ordered UUID (RFC 9562 layout). | **DECISION (04 D9):** primary keys generated in application code (one shared helper per codebase, Node and Dart); columns typed `uuid` with no DB default; the PG 17 image has no native `uuidv7()`. | Never use sequential IDs in URLs/QR (04 D9). UUIDv7 leaks creation time, so it is not a secret: use separate random tokens for links and QR (04 A10/A11). |

Only C0 must come first; the client's B1 ranking can move waves. | Waves are about contracts, not calendar. A Wave-2 chunk can start as soon as its contracts are frozen. |
