# 02 — Security Requirements: Multi-Tenant Cloud LIMS (Nepal)

Status: research baseline, 2026-11-05. Product: multi-tenant LIMS SaaS — Next.js staff app, Node.js service API, self-hosted Supabase (Postgres + Auth + Storage + Realtime), Flutter patient + phlebotomy apps, Windows bridge agent (ASTM/HL7 → cloud ingest), Fonepay/eSewa/Khalti payments, report delivery via WhatsApp/SMS/email + OTP, public QR verification. Data class: real patient health data. Scale: many labs × 500 patients/day × ~20 collection centres.

Legend: `- [ ] REQ-SEC-### | requirement | why | how to verify/test | source`

*Pass 2 phasing note:* the components above are not all Phase 1. Per 01 §6 and 04-consolidated-decisions.md: live payment gateways are Phase 1.5–2 (Phase 1 records payment methods in an append-only ledger, so REQ-SEC-057 applies from day one); Flutter patient/phlebotomy apps are Phase 3 per 01's recommendation, BLOCKED pending client priority. Each requirement applies from the phase in which its component ships; §1, §3, §4, §6, §7 apply from Phase 1.

Nepal legal basis (Individual Privacy Act, 2075 (2018), English translation, ADB copy — https://lpr.adb.org/sites/default/files/resource/%5Bnid%5D/nepal-the-privacy-act-2075-2018-english.pdf):
- s.2(c) defines personal information; s.27(2) defines **sensitive information including physical/mental health**; s.3(1)–(3) protect privacy of physical and mental condition.
- Consent: s.12(2) collection only with consent; s.12(3) use only for original purpose; s.23 authorized collection/processing; s.27(1) restriction on processing sensitive information (exceptions s.27(3)). **Scope note (verified):** s.27(1) is addressed to a **"public body"** only; "public body" (s.2(e)) covers government bodies, state-owned/controlled companies, registered political parties and state-run/state-grant academic institutions — private companies (a separate term "body corporate", s.2(g)) are not covered as such. A private lab or the LIMS provider is therefore bound by s.27(1) only where a tenant is itself a public body (e.g. a government hospital lab).
- Disclosure: s.12(4)(a) **"details relating to health examination" may not be disclosed/published without consent**; s.18 professional confidentiality; s.26(1) no use/sharing by **public bodies and bodies corporate** without consent (this one does bind private labs and the LIMS provider).
- Security duty: s.25(1)–(2) duty to protect against unauthorized access/use/alteration/disclosure/transmission — **addressed to public bodies only** (verified). For private tenants and the LIMS provider, the same controls are adopted here as the means of avoiding s.12(4)/s.18/s.26(1) disclosure offences and s.31 compensation, not as a direct s.25 obligation.
- Liability: s.29(1) offences include breaches of 3(1)/(2)/(4), 12(4), 18, 23(1)/(3)/(7), 26(1), 27(1) (among others); s.29(2) up to 3 years' imprisonment or fine up to NPR 30,000, or both; s.30 complaint to the District Court within 3 months of the act; s.31 District Court may order reasonable compensation to the victim.
- *Pass 3 (Law Commission English text, 05 T12):* s.12(4) ("No person shall, without obtaining the consent of another person, provide … or publish … details relating to health examination") and the s.29 offences ("If a person does, or causes to be done…") are addressed to **any person**, so individual staff (receptionists, phlebotomists, technologists) are personally liable for disclosure; staff training, the acceptable-use policy and access-control rationale must say so.
- **Reading rule for rows below:** wherever a requirement cites "Privacy Act s.25", read it as "s.25 (directly binding for public-body tenants) / disclosure-offence exposure under s.12(4), s.18, s.26(1) and compensation under s.31 (all tenants and the LIMS provider)". (Pass-1 correction; rows not individually rewritten.)
- No data-localisation or cross-border-transfer provision exists in the Act text (verified); residency is a contractual/product constraint (00-scope-baseline §7.1), not a statutory one.
- Privacy Regulation 2077 (2020): elaborates procedures under the Act — **UNVERIFIED section-level content** (official English text not opened; verify with Nepali counsel before relying on it).
- Nepal has **no HIPAA equivalent**; HIPAA Security Rule safeguards (administrative/physical/technical; Rule located at 45 CFR Part 160 and Subparts A and C of Part 164 — verified at https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/index.html; the specific section numbers §164.308/.310/.312 are not on that page — UNVERIFIED — needs confirmation) and ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A are used below as *structure* for lab clients wanting certification, not as legal obligations.

## 1. Multi-tenancy isolation (Postgres RLS + Supabase) — deepest section

Context: tenant = lab; sub-scope = collection centre. API Top 10 2023 ranks BOLA/IDOR #1 (https://owasp.org/API-Security/editions/2023/en/0x11-t10/). Healthcare IDOR is a documented real-world pattern: Australia's ACSC, US CISA and NSA published a joint advisory on IDOR vulnerabilities in web apps (AA23-208A "Preventing Web Application Access Control Abuse", 27 July 2023 — https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2023-07/aa23-208a_joint_csa_preventing_web_application_access_control_abuse.pdf); it is not healthcare-specific but cites IDOR exposure of "health information of millions of users and consumers"; publicly reported examples of per-patient IDOR in health portals exist but specific breach citations below are UNVERIFIED unless linked. Supabase-specific guidance verified at https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security.

### 1.1 RLS policy design

- [ ] REQ-SEC-011 | `ALTER TABLE ... ENABLE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` on **every** table in exposed schemas, including join/link tables, audit tables, and lookup tables holding tenant data; CI check fails if any table lacks RLS | One missed table = full cross-tenant read via PostgREST | CI query against `pg_tables`/`pg_class.relrowsecurity`; fail build on violations | https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security
- [ ] REQ-SEC-012 | Use `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` on tenant tables so a non-privileged table owner is also subject to policies — **and** do not rely on FORCE alone: superusers and roles with `BYPASSRLS` bypass RLS even with FORCE, and on Supabase the default owner `postgres` has `bypassrls`. Therefore no request-serving code path may connect as `postgres`/`supabase_admin`/`service_role` (see REQ-SEC-029/030/131), and where owner-bypass matters, tenant tables are owned by a dedicated NOLOGIN, non-BYPASSRLS owner role | Plain ENABLE exempts the owner; BYPASSRLS roles ignore FORCE entirely | `SELECT relname FROM pg_class WHERE relrowsecurity AND NOT relforcerowsecurity` empty for tenant tables; `SELECT rolname FROM pg_roles WHERE rolbypassrls OR rolsuper` matches the approved list; connection-role audit of every service | https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/ddl-rowsecurity.html ("Superusers and roles with the BYPASSRLS attribute always bypass…"; owner + FORCE); https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security ("On Supabase the owner is postgres, which has bypassrls")
- [ ] REQ-SEC-013 | Every policy filters on `tenant_id` derived from the JWT (`auth.jwt()` claim or membership lookup), never from a client-supplied parameter, header, or request body. *Pass 2 (aligned with 03 §3.1 / REQ-ARCH-019):* all policies call one helper (e.g. `app.current_tenant_id()`) that reads the tenant claim from `request.jwt.claims`; the Fastify API sets `request.jwt.claims` with `set_config(..., true)` (transaction-local) from the JWT it verified itself (REQ-SEC-066), so one policy set serves API, PostgREST, Storage and Realtime. A separate `app.tenant_id` GUC set from request input is not used. Whether self-hosted Storage/Realtime populate `request.jwt.claims` identically is UNVERIFIED — covered by REQ-SEC-040 probes | Client-supplied tenant_id is attacker-supplied | Code review rule; integration test sends forged tenant_id param and expects no effect | https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security
- [ ] REQ-SEC-014 | Write explicit policies for each command (`SELECT`, `INSERT WITH CHECK`, `UPDATE USING + WITH CHECK`, `DELETE`); never rely on a permissive ALL policy alone | Explicit per-command policies keep intent reviewable. Note (verified): if an UPDATE/ALL policy omits WITH CHECK, Postgres reuses its USING expression for the new row, so a correct tenant USING clause already blocks moving a row to another tenant — the risk is a WITH CHECK that is written but *looser* than USING, or multiple permissive policies OR-ing together; state WITH CHECK explicitly and identically-or-stricter | Per-table policy matrix; test moving a row's tenant_id as a normal user fails | PostgreSQL CREATE POLICY docs: https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-createpolicy.html
- [ ] REQ-SEC-015 | Policies name their role with `TO authenticated` (or narrower); no policy targets `public`/`anon` on PHI tables | Supabase guidance: `TO` prevents evaluation for unintended roles; anon must never read PHI | Enumerate `pg_policies` roles in CI | https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security
- [ ] REQ-SEC-016 | Default-deny: with RLS enabled and no matching policy, access is denied — rely on that; never add a catch-all `USING (true)` policy "temporarily" | One `true` policy nullifies isolation | grep policies for `(true)` in CI; manual review of pg_policies dump in every release | PostgreSQL RLS docs (default-deny semantics)
- [ ] REQ-SEC-017 | Collection-centre scoping: staff restricted to their centre(s) via membership table checked in policies, in addition to tenant_id | 20 centres/lab; phlebotomist at centre A must not browse centre B patients | Cross-centre probe test in CI | design requirement (API1 BOLA mitigation)
- [ ] REQ-SEC-018 | No PHI in any table/column exposed to `anon`; the public QR-verify path uses a dedicated minimal view/function (see REQ-SEC-093) | anon key ships in clients and is public | Attempt anon SELECT on every exposed table in CI | https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security

### 1.2 SECURITY DEFINER and search_path

- [ ] REQ-SEC-019 | Every `SECURITY DEFINER` function sets `SET search_path = ''` and schema-qualifies all object references | Owner (postgres) has bypassrls; an unqualified name can be hijacked via caller's search_path → privilege escalation | Lint: `SELECT proname FROM pg_proc WHERE prosecdef AND proconfig IS NULL` (or lacking search_path) must be empty | https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security ; https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-createfunction.html#SQL-CREATEFUNCTION-SECURITY
- [ ] REQ-SEC-020 | SECURITY DEFINER functions live in a schema **not exposed** via the Data API, take no caller-controlled tenant parameter, and are individually reviewed; keep an inventory | Each one is a deliberate RLS bypass; Supabase docs warn against exposing them | Inventory + review sign-off per function; CI diff on pg_proc prosecdef set | https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security
- [ ] REQ-SEC-021 | Prefer SECURITY INVOKER + explicit grants; use DEFINER only where a policy needs a cross-table lookup that would otherwise recurse (error 42P17) | Minimizes bypass surface | Justification recorded per DEFINER function | https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security

### 1.3 RLS performance (so nobody is tempted to turn it off)

- [ ] REQ-SEC-022 | Wrap stable functions in policies — `(SELECT auth.uid())`, `(SELECT auth.jwt())` — so Postgres caches them per statement instead of per row | Verified Supabase guidance; unwrapped calls make RLS orders of magnitude slower on 500-patients/day tables | EXPLAIN ANALYZE on hot queries shows InitPlan, not per-row function calls | https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security
- [ ] REQ-SEC-023 | B-tree index on every column a policy filters on — at minimum `tenant_id` on every tenant table (leading column of composite indexes) | Verified Supabase guidance; policy predicates without indexes cause sequential scans | Index audit vs pg_policies predicates | https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security
- [ ] REQ-SEC-024 | Add the tenant filter explicitly in application queries as well as in RLS ("defence in depth + planner help") | Redundant filter both documents intent and lets the planner prune; RLS stays the enforcement layer | Query-layer convention enforced in code review | engineering requirement — the current Supabase RLS page (opened 2026-11-05) gives index + `(select …)` wrapping guidance but no separate "add filters" tip; UNVERIFIED — needs confirmation as an external citation
- [ ] REQ-SEC-025 | Load-test RLS policies with production-shaped data (millions of result rows across tenants) before launch; budget p95 latency for the 10 hottest queries | RLS performance regressions are the top reason teams weaken policies | k6/pgbench run in staging per release | engineering requirement (UNVERIFIED as external citation)

### 1.4 Cross-tenant referential integrity

- [ ] REQ-SEC-026 | Composite keys/FKs: child tables reference `(tenant_id, id)` of the parent (parent has `UNIQUE (tenant_id, id)`), so a row can never point at another tenant's parent | Plain FK on id alone allows an INSERT referencing a foreign tenant's order/patient even with RLS on reads, because FK/unique checks always bypass RLS (verified) — which also makes FK/unique violations a covert existence-oracle across tenants. Postgres docs also warn of covert-channel leaks through referential-integrity checks → keep unique constraints tenant-scoped (UNIQUE (tenant_id, …)). | Attempt cross-tenant FK insert in CI; must fail with FK violation | https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/ddl-rowsecurity.html ("Referential integrity checks … always bypass row security … covert channel"); composite-FK multi-tenant pattern is engineering practice (no specific external link — UNVERIFIED — needs confirmation)
- [ ] REQ-SEC-027 | `tenant_id NOT NULL` with no default pointing at a real tenant; set via trigger/claim, CHECK against JWT tenant on insert | NULL or defaulted tenant_id rows fall outside policies or into the wrong tenant | Schema lint in CI | design requirement
- [ ] REQ-SEC-028 | Sequences/IDs: use UUIDv4 (or UUIDv7 for locality) for externally visible identifiers; never sequential integers in URLs or QR tokens | Sequential IDs make IDOR enumeration trivial (API1 BOLA) | grep routes for integer-id params; pentest enumeration attempt | https://owasp.org/API-Security/editions/2023/en/0x11-t10/

### 1.5 Supabase-specific gotchas

- [ ] REQ-SEC-029 | `service_role` — i.e. the new-style **secret key** (`SUPABASE_SECRET_KEY`) and the legacy JWT `service_role` key, plus any key/role with bypassrls — is server-side only: never in browser, Flutter app, bridge agent, or client-reachable config; rotate on any suspected exposure | service_role bypasses all RLS — it is the master key to every tenant's PHI | Secret scanning on repos/builds; mobile APK/IPA string scan; CSP/report of key use from browser | https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security
- [ ] REQ-SEC-030 | Server code using service_role sets the tenant context explicitly and is confined to a short list of audited modules; everything else uses the user's JWT so RLS applies | Note (verified): when a request carries a user access token, RLS applies even on a secret-key client — use that property deliberately | Module allowlist; code review gate | https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security
- [ ] REQ-SEC-031 | All views on tenant tables created `WITH (security_invoker = true)` (Postgres 15+); CI blocks views without it in exposed schemas | Views run as owner by default and silently bypass RLS | `SELECT ... FROM pg_class/pg_options` audit in CI | https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security
- [ ] REQ-SEC-032 | Expose only a curated schema (e.g. `api`) through PostgREST; keep raw tables, DEFINER functions, and internal schemas unexposed | Shrinks the anonymous/authenticated attack surface; Supabase "exposed schemas" setting | Config review: exposed schemas list = {api} (PostgREST `db-schemas`, default `public`) | https://supabase.com/docs/guides/api/securing-your-api ("Internal tables and helper functions remain in schemas that aren't exposed"); https://docs.postgrest.org/en/stable/references/configuration.html (`db-schemas`)
- [ ] REQ-SEC-033 | Constrain PostgREST query shape: set `db-max-rows` (hard row cap); PostgREST has **no configuration setting for embedding depth or disabling filters** (verified), so restrict traversal by exposing only curated views/RPC functions in the `api` schema (no raw PHI tables with FK relationships exposed), optional Supabase pre-request checks to deny direct access to specific relations, and edge (nginx) limits on query-string length (*Pass 2: the self-hosted stack's internal gateway is Envoy by default, Kong optional — 03 §2.1*); prefer RPC endpoints for complex PHI reads | Resource embedding lets a caller traverse any FK relationship of an exposed relation; unlimited rows enables bulk exfiltration | Config review (`db-max-rows` set); probe with deep `select=` embeddings in tests | https://docs.postgrest.org/en/stable/references/configuration.html (`db-max-rows`: "A hard limit to the number of rows PostgREST will fetch…"); https://supabase.com/docs/guides/api/securing-your-api (pre-request checks)
- [ ] REQ-SEC-034 | Storage: one bucket per purpose, private by default; bucket RLS policies on `storage.objects` scope by tenant prefix (`tenant_id/...` path convention) and role; no public buckets for reports/TRFs | Storage policies are separate from table RLS and commonly forgotten; public buckets are readable by anyone regardless of policies (verified), so a public bucket of PDFs is a mass breach; service key bypasses Storage RLS entirely | Automated probe: fetch another tenant's object path with each role | https://supabase.com/docs/guides/storage/security/access-control (verified: RLS on `storage.objects`, default deny for uploads without policies, `storage.foldername(name)` path helper, public buckets "already publicly accessible")
- [ ] REQ-SEC-035 | Signed URLs for report downloads: short expiry (minutes–hours, matched to OTP session), generated per request, never stored in logs or sent over unauthenticated channels without the OTP step | A leaked long-lived signed URL is an unauthenticated PHI leak; verified: individual signed URLs **cannot be revoked** — only by deleting/moving/renaming the object or rotating the URL signing key (which invalidates all), and they stay valid through Auth JWT key rotation — so TTL is the main control; incident runbook must include signing-key rotation | Check TTL config; grep logs for signed URL query strings; runbook drill rotates the signing key | https://supabase.com/docs/guides/storage/serving/downloads
- [ ] REQ-SEC-036 | Realtime: disable "Allow public access" so only private channels (`config: { private: true }`) work; write `select`/`insert` RLS policies on `realtime.messages` scoped by `realtime.topic()` and `extension` (broadcast/presence) per tenant; Postgres Changes are filtered by the subscribed table's own RLS, so REQ-SEC-011..016 must hold for any table in the publication; never broadcast PHI on channels joinable with anon key; channel names must not encode PHI; keep JWT expiry short because channel authorization is evaluated at join and cached for the connection until a new token is sent or it expires | Broadcast/Presence authorization is opt-in (public channels are joinable by anyone with the anon key); revocation lags until JWT refresh | Subscribe with anon key and with a foreign-tenant JWT to every channel pattern in CI; expect deny | https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/authorization (verified; page has no self-hosting section — confirm the same settings exist in the self-hosted Realtime version in use: UNVERIFIED — needs confirmation)
- [ ] REQ-SEC-037 | Self-hosted Supabase: replace ALL placeholder secrets from `.env.example` before first start (JWT secret / signing keys, publishable + secret keys — legacy anon/service_role keys if used — `POSTGRES_PASSWORD`, `DASHBOARD_USERNAME`/`DASHBOARD_PASSWORD`, SMTP), keep them in a secrets manager, terminate TLS at a reverse proxy in front of the gateway, keep Studio/pg-meta off the public internet (Studio's only built-in protection is HTTP basic auth, served on port 8000 over plain HTTP by default), keep the API gateway config (Envoy default, Kong optional — Pass 2, per 03 §2.1) under version control | Supabase docs: "you should never start your self-hosted Supabase using these defaults"; basic auth over HTTP on the internet is not adequate for an admin console holding PHI. (Claim that default-secret deployments "have been mass-compromised": UNVERIFIED — needs confirmation; no incident source found) | External scan: Studio/meta endpoints unreachable; secrets differ from `.env.example` | https://supabase.com/docs/guides/self-hosting/docker (verified 2026-11-05)

### 1.6 Tenant-isolation testing

- [ ] REQ-SEC-040 | Automated cross-tenant probe suite in CI: for every API route and PostgREST table, authenticate as tenant A and request tenant B's known object IDs (read, write, delete, FK-reference); any 2xx/leak fails the build | IDOR/BOLA is API Top 10 #1; only continuous testing catches regressions | Suite runs on every merge; coverage report lists untested routes | https://owasp.org/API-Security/editions/2023/en/0x11-t10/

## 8. Top 20 most likely real-world failure modes for exactly this product (ranked)

1. **Cross-tenant IDOR through a missed RLS policy or owner-bypass.** A new table (say, `report_comments`) ships without a policy, or a migration role owns the table without FORCE RLS; any authenticated user of any lab reads every lab's rows through PostgREST. Found by a curious lab's IT person or a researcher; instant multi-lab PHI breach. (API1 BOLA — https://owasp.org/API-Security/editions/2023/en/0x11-t10/)
2. **service_role key leak.** The key lands in a Flutter build, a client-side fetch, a public repo, or the bridge-agent config "to make ingest easier". Whoever extracts it bypasses all RLS and dumps every tenant's patients. One string = total breach.
3. **Report-link/OTP brute force.** Short tokens or 4-digit OTPs with no attempt limits on the public report endpoint; a script walks tokens/OTPs and downloads thousands of PDF reports. The public, unauthenticated surface is exactly where rate limits get forgotten.
4. **Public or misconfigured Storage bucket.** Report PDFs/TRF scans in a bucket with a permissive policy or guessable public URLs; a crawler or ex-employee enumerates paths. Storage policies are configured separately from table RLS and are the most commonly forgotten layer in Supabase apps.
5. **Forged payment callback / trusting the redirect.** The app marks orders paid from the success-redirect parameters without the Khalti lookup/eSewa status check; attackers (or savvy patients) replay or forge callbacks and get free testing — then the same gap gets scripted. (Verified: Khalti lookup returns HTTP 200 for Pending/Initiated/Refunded/Partially Refunded as well as Completed — https://docs.khalti.com/khalti-epayment/)
6. **Self-hosted Supabase shipped with defaults.** Default JWT secret/dashboard password, or Studio/pg-meta exposed to the internet; mass scanners find it within days. Self-hosted default-secret compromises are a recurring public pattern.
7. **Stored XSS from patient-controlled text into staff UI → staff session theft.** A "patient name" or remark containing a payload renders unescaped in the Next.js staff app or in a generated/merged PDF viewer; attacker rides a receptionist's session into the whole tenant.
8. **Phlebotomy phone lost/stolen with unencrypted offline cache.** The field app caches the day's patients (names, phones, tests) in plaintext SQLite; a stolen phone is a 50-patient breach plus Privacy Act s.29 exposure, and it will happen at the scale of 20 centres × many labs.
9. **Bridge-agent token theft from a lab PC.** Commodity infostealer on the reception PC grabs a plaintext device token; if the ingest API is not least-privilege, the attacker reads patient data back or injects false results. Even least-privilege, result injection/poisoning remains possible — hence server-side plausibility checks.
10. **Insider at a lab exporting data.** A staff account with broad read access exports patient lists (phone numbers are valuable to marketers/competitors). No per-role export limits, no volume alerting → discovered only when patients complain about spam calls.
11. **Webhook/callback replay double-crediting or OTP/SMS flooding.** Missing idempotency lets retried gateway callbacks or re-sent OTP requests double-fire effects — duplicate receipts, SMS cost blowouts, or a patient's report unlocked without payment.
12. **PHI leakage through delivery channels.** Result details pasted into WhatsApp/SMS templates, lock-screen previews, or wrong-number delivery (one digit off at registration). Legally this is s.12(4) disclosure; operationally it is the most frequent small breach a lab will generate.
13. **SECURITY DEFINER function with mutable search_path or exposed via API.** A helper function written to dodge an RLS recursion error becomes a tenant-crossing oracle or privilege-escalation gadget. (Verified risk — https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security)
14. **Views without security_invoker leaking across tenants.** A reporting view created for dashboards runs as owner and ignores RLS; the analytics page happily shows another tenant's aggregates — then someone notices the underlying rows are queryable too.
15. **Realtime channel broadcasting PHI to anon.** Live "new result" updates on a channel joinable with the public anon key; anyone who reads the JS bundle subscribes to the firehose.
16. **PDF pipeline SSRF/parser exploit.** The report renderer fetches an attacker-supplied logo URL (SSRF into the DC network) or the merge step parses a malicious outsourced PDF that exploits the library — code execution on the box that holds everything.
17. **Credential stuffing of staff accounts.** Lab staff reuse passwords; no MFA, weak lockout → one tenant-admin takeover = that lab's entire patient base. Admin MFA is the single cheapest mitigation on this list.
18. **Backup/staging copies breached.** Unencrypted pg_dump on a developer laptop, or prod data restored into a soft staging environment reachable with test credentials. Secondary copies get none of the primary's controls unless forced.
19. **Ransomware/data destruction with untested restores.** The DB host is encrypted by an intruder (often via exposed SSH/RDP); backups exist but were never restore-tested or sit on the same host and are encrypted too. Labs lose legally required records; the business may not survive it.
20. **Supply-chain compromise of a dependency or the agent updater.** A poisoned npm package in the Node API or an unsigned bridge-agent auto-update channel pushes attacker code to every lab simultaneously — OWASP Top 10:2025's new A03 made this a headline category for a reason (https://top10.owasp.org/2025/).
